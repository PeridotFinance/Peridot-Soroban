# Margin slippage: XLM/USDC, Aquarius vs Reflector (25 Nov 2026)

Read-only. The only calls used were simulateTransaction, getLedgerEntries, getEvents and getLedgers on `soroban-rpc.mainnet.stellar.gateway.fm`, plus Horizon GETs. There were no keys, no signing and no repo edits.

Scripts and raw output are in this folder:
- `probe.cjs` and `samples/s-<ledger>.json`: the live 5-minute samples.
- `forkctx.cjs` / `forkctx.json`: pool events and the classic DEX around the fork. Its Reflector field is empty, so `refhist.cjs` was used for the oracle history.
- `refhist.cjs`: the Reflector per-round history.
- `history.cjs` / `history-65661687.json`: 20 h of real Reflector against the pool mid.
- `tail7d.cjs` / `tail7d.json`: a 7-day proxy.
- `multihop.cjs` and `otherdex.cjs`: the other routes.
- `rates.cjs`: the borrow rates.
- `aggregate.py` / `aggregate.json`: the cost table.

All bps figures are measured against the oracle as the controller reads it: `get_price_usd(XLM) / get_price_usd(USDC)`, from Reflector `CAFJZQ…` with `Other(XLM)` and `Other(USDC)` in a USD base. Negative means the trader gets less than the oracle value.

## 1. Decomposition

### 1a. At the fork ledger 65661251 (close 1795592312 = 07:38:32 UTC)

These numbers come from the WP4a `fork.json`, `fetch.log`, and from pool events and the Reflector `price(asset, ts)` history.

- **Reflector, as read at the fork:** round ts 1795592100 (07:35:00), so it was **212 s old**.
  - XLM $0.12389259, USDC $1.00117992.
  - Implied price: **0.123747 USDC/XLM**.
- **Pool:** slot0 sqrt_price_x96 27741203905149717774899785844, tick −20990.
  - Mid **0.122601 USDC/XLM**, which is **−92.6 bps vs the oracle**.
  - Last pool trade at ledger 65661250 (07:38:27), 5 s before the fork.
- **What happened (pool_state events):**
  - The pool mid fell from 0.12363 (07:36:17) to 0.12256 (07:37:52), about −0.9 % in 95 s.
  - The classic DEX shows the same drop: the 07:35 5-min candle moved 0.12358 → 0.12291 on 191k XLM of volume.
  - Reflector still carried the pre-drop 07:35 round.
  - The next round (07:40:00) printed 0.122900, closing most of the gap. The pool had already rebounded to 0.12274 by then.
- **Identity from the two fork legs:**
  - The open bought XLM with USDC and came out **+83 bps** vs the oracle.
  - The close sold XLM and came out **−102 bps**.
  - Half the sum, (+83 − 102)/2 = **−9.5 bps**, is fee plus impact. That is the 10 bps fee with ≈0 impact.
  - Half the difference, (−83 − 102)/2 = **−92.5 bps**, is the mid offset. It matches slot0.
- **Fork quotes:**
  - 15 USDC → 122.2256512 XLM.
  - 120 XLM → 14.6972230 USDC.
  - 1,200 XLM → 146.9634316 USDC.
  - 12,000 XLM → 1,468.7549629 USDC.
  - The quote rate is almost flat across sizes, so impact was negligible.
- **Verdict:** depth contributed nothing and the fee was 10 bps. **About 90 % of the gap was Reflector lag during a fast −0.9 % move.**
- **That minute was unusual.** It was the single worst minute in the 20 h real-data window (−96 bps at 07:38).

### 1d. Answer

The gap is **mainly a standing offset from oracle lag**. Reflector publishes 5-min rounds about 15–45 s after each round timestamp, so the oracle seen by a trade is 0–5.5 min old. The offset is roughly the XLM move since that timestamp. Normally it is under 35 bps, about 1 % at the p99.7 level, and up to about 3 % on the worst minute of a week.

The **fee is a constant 10 bps per leg**. **Depth is negligible** at pilot size (<0.6 bps) and small at Tier 1 (2,000–2,500 dollar legs: 9–11 bps).

Failures are adversely selected. A long's close fails after a sharp drop, and a short's close fails after a sharp rally. Those are exactly the moments when users want out.

## 3. Round-trip cost

The swap legs use the swap sizes from the WP2 margin code (`.wt-margin-wp2/.../margin-controller/src/contract.rs`):
- open long swaps `borrow − fee` USDC;
- open short swaps the `borrow` XLM;
- close swaps the held asset needed to repay the debt.

So each leg is about the **debt D = (L−1)·E**, not the notional.

Other assumptions:
- Fees are 0.10 % + 0.10 % of gross notional L·E.
- Funding is +2 % APR on D.
- The cash-market borrow rate is read live from the receipts' `BorrowYearlyRateScaled`: **USDC 8.0 %, XLM 6.0 %**. These are fixed; no rate model is set.
- Swap cost = 2 × 10 bps fee + impact(open) + impact(close), using the mean impact over the 8 full-grid samples. The standing offset cancels over a round trip if it is the same at open and close; its mean was +1.4 bps.
- Long = USDC debt; short = XLM debt.
- The close actually sells D + fees + funding with a `(1 + slip)` buffer. The extra is converted and returned as surplus, adding about 0.1–0.2 bp of equity. This is ignored.

**Spread vs Reflector.** In expectation it adds nothing to the round trip, because it cancels across the two legs. As a per-leg risk, a p99 offset of 63 bps on D is ±1.3 % of equity at 3× and ±2.5 % at 5×; the sign is random.

**Margin left by a 1.5 % oracle floor**, per leg, after the fee and impact:
- pilot (D ≤ $100): 150 − 10.4 = **139.6 bps** available for lag;
- Tier 1 at 3× (D = $1,000): 150 − 14.5 = **135.5 bps**;
- Tier 1 at 5× (D = $2,000): 150 − 19 = **131 bps**.

The 1 % floor leaves only 81–90 bps, which was exceeded at the fork.

## 4. Recommendation

**1.5 % is a reasonable `MaxSlippage` for the pilot and for Tier 1.**
- Depth is not the constraint: under 0.6 bps at pilot size and at most 11 bps at Tier 1.
- What 1.5 % does is raise the oracle-lag tolerance from about 85 bps to about 135 bps.
- Real 20 h data: a 1.5 % floor would have rejected **0** minutes. The 1 % floor rejects 0.17 %, including the fork minute.
- 7-day proxy: 1.5 % rejects about **0.09 % of minutes (≈9 min a week, 4–5 episodes)**. 1 % rejects about 0.28 % (≈28 min a week, 11 episodes).
- Rejections remain in the very fastest minutes, and they hit the side that wants to exit.
- A rejected `close` does not trap the trader:
  - `close_with_repay` has no swap;
  - liquidation has no swap (WP4a fork result: "repay, seize + remainder pToken transfers; no swap");
  - waiting one round almost always clears it.
- So 1.5 % is safe as an availability setting. Going above about 2 % buys little, because the remaining rejections are 2–3 % moves, and it raises the cost of pool manipulation.

**Side effects of 1.5 % in the WP2 code.** `MaxSlippage` is also the solvency term and the close-sell buffer:
- `liq_ltv × 1.04 × (1 + slip) < 1` becomes 0.90 × 1.04 × 1.015 = **0.950 < 1** at 5×. It still passes; the limit is slip ≈6.8 %.
- `check_open_ltv` and the pre-trade LTV use `oracle_min_out`, so they become slightly stricter at 1.5 %.
- The close sells about 1.5 % more of the held asset than the debt and returns the surplus. That is harmless but adds a little fee volume.
- SPEC §4 hard-codes 1 % in two places. With 1.5 % they become:
  - `close_reserve_usd` 1.01× → **1.015×**;
  - the §4 table's "4.96C" → 4.94C.
- The "~5.5 % gap tolerated" figure at 5× shrinks by about 0.5 pp.

**Should the floor be measured against the pool's own quote instead of Reflector? Not as a replacement.**

For a pool-quote floor:
- It removes false rejections from oracle lag.
- It still protects against MEV and sandwiching within the transaction, as a `min_out` would.

Against it:
- Within one transaction the pool's quote is the pool's own state, so it gives **no protection against a manipulated or drained pool**.
- In a pooled-margin design the loss from a bad fill goes beyond the trader's equity into backstop or pooled debt. Opens in particular could be used to push value out of the pool against the backstop.
- Reflector is the only independent reference the contract has.

Suggested design:
- **Opens:** keep the Reflector floor at 1.5 %, and add a user `min_out`. Opens can simply wait for the next round.
- **Closes:**
  - keep the Reflector floor at 1.5 %, or widen to about 2–2.5 % for closes only, since a close can only reduce exposure;
  - plus the user's `min_out`, which the code already supports via `.max(min_out)`;
  - plus `close_with_repay` as the always-available fallback.
- Optional: a defensive blend, `floor = max(oracle × (1 − slip), poolQuote × (1 − 0.3 %))`. Never accept a quote below the oracle floor just because the pool agrees with it.
- Optional: a keeper or front end that tells users to retry a rejected close after the next Reflector round (≤5 min).

## Caveats

- Reflector history is only available for about 20 h on chain. The 7-day tail is a calibrated proxy (correlation 0.86, slightly understating), not real Reflector.
- 45 minutes of sampling covers about 9 oracle rounds. Day-of-week and volatility-regime effects are not captured; the SPEC's 12-month 5-min statistics are the better tail reference.
- Impact figures are `estimate_swap` simulations against the live state, not executed fills.
- One live sample (08:52) failed with an RPC 429.
